1.1 Emails as Modern Handwritten Letters

Emails replaced handwritten letters.

Our emails are the modern version of that.
We are not “sending emails.”

When you write, you are entering their world — their excitement, their planning, their daydreaming.
This is why our tone works.

You do not “write” emails.
You build them.
Just like Nikola does.
You build them:
* block by block
* sentence by sentence
* idea by idea
* not in order
* not in one go
* not from top to bottom
You assemble the pieces:
* greeting
* warmth
* route suggestion
* boat recommendation
* captain introduction
* amenities
* pricing
* inclusions

* concierge touches
* closing
Then you rearrange them until the flow feels right.
This removes pressure.

This makes the tone consistent.
This makes the email feel natural.
You are not performing.
You are constructing.
This is how Nikola writes.
This is how you will write.
This is how MYH maintains its voice.

Every MYH email is made of blocks.

* You add what strengthens the message.

* You refine the tone.
Common blocks include:
* Warm greeting
* “My name is…” introduction
* Acknowledging their message
* Route recommendation

Examples of MYH rhythm:

Notice:
* no exclamation overload
* no unnecessary adjectives

3.4 Personalization Without Templates
We do not use templates.

* mention their group size

3.5 Sparse Use of Exclamation Marks
This is a core MYH rule.
Long emails:
Use 1–2 exclamation marks max.
Shorter emails:
Use 1–2 across the entire thread.
Why:

When to use them:

* enthusiasm

Examples of correct usage:
* “Sounds like a great trip!”
* “Happy to help!”
Examples of incorrect usage:
* “We can’t wait to have you!!!”
* “This will be AMAZING!!!”
Exclamation marks are seasoning, not sauce.

SECTION 4 — HANDLING INCOMPLETE INQUIRIES (THE MYH SIGNATURE MOVE)
How to turn vague, missing‑info inquiries into high‑conversion conversations.
4.1 Why We Never Ask for Missing Info Up Front
This is one of the most powerful psychological techniques in the entire MYH system.
When a guest sends an incomplete inquiry — missing:
* dates
* group size
* preferred route
* pickup location
* duration
* number of boats
* or anything else
We do NOT reply with a short “Can you send me the dates?” message.
Why?
Because:
* It kills momentum.
* It feels transactional.
* It feels like work for them.
* It feels like we’re not invested.
* It feels like a form they forgot to fill out.
* It gives them no emotional reward.
* It gives them nothing to respond to.
And worst of all:
It wastes the most valuable moment — their initial excitement.
Instead, we do the opposite.
We reward their inquiry with a full, warm, premium reply — even if we don’t have all the details yet.
This is the MYH signature move.
4.2 How to Build a Full Email Around Missing Details
Even if the inquiry is missing key info, you still write the full email:
* warm greeting
* “My name is…” introduction
* route suggestion
* boat recommendation
* captain introduction
* amenities
* inclusions
* exclusions
* links
* photos
* concierge touches
* tone
* warmth
* local insight
You build the entire experience as if you already know the date.
You make them feel:
* taken care of
* guided
* understood
* excited
* welcomed
* safe
Only at the end do you politely ask for the missing detail.
This is how you turn a vague inquiry into a high‑intent conversation.
4.3 The Psychology Behind This Technique
Here’s why this works so well:
1. You create emotional investment.
By the time they reach the end of your email, they’re already imagining:
* the boat
* the route
* the captain
* the swim stops
* the lunch
* the wine tasting
* the day on the sea
They’re already “in.”
2. You reward their inquiry.
Most operators reply with:
“Please send dates.”
We reply with:
“Here’s what your day could look like.”
This feels premium.
3. You make them feel taken care of.
They feel like you’ve already done work for them.
4. You remove friction.
Instead of asking them to do something first, you give them something first.
5. You increase reply rates dramatically.
People respond to value, not requests.
6. You position MYH as a hospitality brand.
Not a rental company.
Not a form‑processing machine.
A concierge.
This technique alone will make Ivano dangerous in the best way — because it transforms vague inquiries into booked trips.
4.4 How to Ask for Missing Info at the End
This is the key:
You ask for the missing detail only after delivering value.
And you ask politely, calmly, and with purpose.
Examples:
* “Just let me know the date, and I’ll confirm availability and send you the exact quote.”
* “Once I know your group size, I can recommend the best boat for comfort.”
* “If you tell me the date, I’ll check availability and finalize the price for you.”
* “When you have a moment, just send me the date — I’ll take care of the rest.”
Notice:
* no pressure
* no urgency
* no guilt
* no “I can’t help you unless…”
* no “please send…”
* no “I need…”
It’s calm.
It’s premium.
It’s human.
4.5 Examples of Perfect Incomplete‑Inquiry Replies
Example 1 — Missing Date
Guest:  
“Hi, we’re 4 people looking for a private boat day in Hvar. What are your options?”
MYH Reply:  
Warm greeting → introduction → route suggestion → boat recommendation → captain intro → amenities → pricing logic (but no exact price yet) → concierge touches → ask for date at the end.
Ending line:  
“Just let me know the date you’re considering, and I’ll confirm availability and send you the exact price in EUR and your currency.”
Example 2 — Missing Group Size
Guest:  
“Hi, can you send me options for a private tour to Vis?”
MYH Reply:  
Warm greeting → route description → boat options (general) → captain intro → amenities → inclusions → exclusions → concierge touches.
Ending line:  
“Just let me know how many people are in your group, and I’ll recommend the best boat and send the exact price.”
Example 3 — Missing Everything (Very Vague Inquiry)
Guest:  
“Hi, can you send me info about your tours?”
MYH Reply:  
Warm greeting → introduction → two route options → two boat options → captain intro → amenities → links → concierge touches.
Ending line:  
“If you tell me your date and group size, I’ll check availability and send you the exact quote in EUR and your currency.”
Example 4 — Missing Pickup Location
Guest:  
“We’re staying near Split and want to visit Hvar. What do you recommend?”
MYH Reply:  
Warm greeting → introduction → route suggestion → boat recommendation → amenities → eFoil upsell if relevant → concierge touches.
Ending line:  
“Just let me know where exactly you’re staying, and I’ll confirm pickup options and send you the exact price.”

When something goes wrong, MYH communication follows four principles:
1. Calm first
Never match the guest’s stress.

This creates psychological safety.
